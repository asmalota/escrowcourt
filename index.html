Money in escrow. Two sides. A jury that reads the evidence.

A buyer escrows real GEN into the contract when opening a dispute, so the amount is custody rather than a number in a description. The named seller opts in; until they do, the buyer can pull the escrow back. Both sides then file evidence URLs, and an on-chain jury fetches those pages and rules how the escrow is released — full refund, full payment, or a split. The contract pays out with a real transfer, and a second settle is refused. The verdict is a pair — a winner and a share — and the two must agree: buyer is exactly 100% to the buyer, seller is exactly 0%, and split must sit strictly between. Anything contradictory is refused by the contract itself, and check_verdict exposes that rule so it can be audited without running consensus.

How it works

  1. create_dispute — payable. Your GEN is escrowed. Buyer and seller must differ.
  2. accept_dispute — seller only. Until they accept, refund_if_unaccepted lets the buyer reclaim everything.
  3. submit_evidence — either party, a URL plus a description. The jury reads the fetched pages, not the volume of text.
  4. request_ruling — the jury fetches every evidence URL and returns a winner and a share. Each validator re-runs the jury and must reach the same outcome; a split that drifts by more than 10 points is rejected.
  5. settle — pays the escrow out with a real transfer, once.
The verdict rule is public: check_verdict(winner, split_percent, leader_winner, leader_percent) returns the same boolean consensus stops on. The contract also refuses a malformed payload and a payload whose winner contradicts its own percentage.
